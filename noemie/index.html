Reading the numbers

Small changes between two tests are often measurement noise: a different wall mark, a slow thumb on the timer. Look for the trend across weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12. Jumps should climb and sprint times should drop, but not every test will beat the last one, especially while she's growing fast.

Rules for every session

  1. Two days a week. At least one day apart, about 45 minutes each, never the day before a game. This replaces extra pickup or extra drills; it doesn't stack on top of them. If basketball was hard that day, do the warm-up, the lifts and the core work, and skip the sprints, slides and jumps.
  2. Speed work is all-out with full rest. Walk back after every sprint and rest before the next one. Stop a set when she slows down or the landings get loud.
  3. Lifts stop 2 or 3 reps short. She's strong already, so the weights stay moderate and the energy goes to speed. Go up to the next dumbbell or kettlebell only when every set is clean at the top of the rep range.
  4. On the balls of the feet. In slides, backpedals, pogos and jump rope her heels stay light, knees bent and hips low.
  5. Landings. Quiet feet, knees over the toes and never caving in, hips back, chest up. On "stick" reps she holds the landing for 2 s. If the knees cave in, go back to the easier version until they don't. Girls in their growth years tend to land and cut with the knees caving in, a common setup for knee injuries, so every session drills it.
  6. Pain rule. Answer the pain check at the end of every session. Any pain in the knees (especially below the kneecap), heels, shins or back means no sprints, slides or jumps that day; finish the upper body and core only. Pain two sessions in a row, pain when walking, or pain that wakes her at night: stop and see a doctor or physical therapist before the next session.
  7. Growth check. Measure height on every test day. If she grew 1/4 in or more since the last check, the growth flag turns on: depth jumps in weeks 9 to 12 become box jumps, and nothing gets added. Kids often look clumsy during fast growth; keep the reps clean and patient.
  8. Depth jumps are earned. They turn on in week 9 only if all 8 sessions in weeks 5 to 8 have a pain check of No and the growth flag is off. A pain check left blank counts against it. The app makes the swap automatically.
  9. An adult is there for every session, watching landings and lifts, and running the partner drills. Weeks 1 and 2 are for learning each move.
  10. Fuel and sleep. 9 to 12 hours of sleep a night. A real meal with protein within a couple of hours after training, a snack before practice, and water through the day. This plan has no weight goals and no food rules: growing athletes need more fuel, not less.

S-Drive and rainy days

Sled pushes run on the S-Drive in sled mode. Start at a low level and go up one only when her lean and stride look the same as the level before. On a rainy day the outdoor sprints and ball drops move to the S-Drive in harness mode at a light parachute level: same number of reps, 4 to 5 s each, full rest. Slides, drop steps, backpedals, mirror drills and the 5-10-5 need real ground, so do them indoors if there's room or skip them that day. Before her first rep, adjust the harness to fit her and check that she can reach the push bar with her body leaned and arms bent.

Lighter weeks and tests

Weeks 4, 8 and 12 drop one set from everything. Day B of weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12 is test day: warm up, run the tests, then that phase's lifts at 2 sets. Log results in the Tests tab.

Not in this plan

Max-weight lifts, high boxes, long jump circuits, distance running for conditioning, Olympic lifts, and any weight or size goals. Basketball already covers her conditioning.
